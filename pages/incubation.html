How to get started

Every project begins somewhere. Here is one way to move from an initial idea to practical action.

1. Identify a problem

Explore challenges facing Somalia and identify problems that are important, neglected, and possible to address.

2. Explore solutions

Review the available evidence, learn from existing efforts, and consider which approaches have the greatest potential to make a difference.

3. Develop your idea

Define your goals, speak with relevant communities, test your assumptions, and make a practical plan.

4. Take action

Start small, learn from the results, improve your approach, and explore partnerships or funding as your project develops.

Problems worth solving

We are especially interested in neglected problems where evidence-backed action could improve many lives or substantially reduce suffering. You can explore these areas or propose another important problem that deserves more attention.

Animal welfare

Develop practical ways to reduce animal suffering and improve the treatment of animals in farming and other systems.

Global health and development

Address preventable illness, early death, poverty, and other barriers to human flourishing through effective interventions.

AI safety and governance

Explore ways to make AI systems safer and ensure that their development and use benefit people and communities in Somalia.

Your idea does not have to fit neatly into one of these areas. We are also interested in other promising approaches to important and neglected problems.

Who can start a project?

Anyone with a genuine interest in solving important problems can explore an idea. You might be a student, researcher, doctor, engineer, lawyer, veterinarian, community organiser, or someone with a completely different background.

You don't need

  • Previous non-profit experience
  • An established organisation
  • A fully developed project idea
  • A specific degree

It helps to have

  • A desire to make a positive difference
  • Curiosity about important problems
  • A willingness to learn from evidence
  • Persistence and openness to feedback
