Our context

The questions that matter here

Somalia has a young, capable population and a diaspora that already moves enormous sums home every year. Where that talent and money goes is a decision worth making carefully.

Diaspora giving

Two billion dollars a year, already moving

The US State Department estimates remittances to Somalia at around $2 billion annually — by its estimate roughly a quarter of GDP — with most of it moving through hawalas and mobile money. Small improvements in where and how it lands add up to a great deal.

Source: US State Department, 2025 Investment Climate Statement. Other estimates put the GDP share lower; the dollar figure is widely cited.

Food security

Drought, and the gap between responses

Recurring drought puts millions at risk. Some responses are far better studied and far cheaper per life improved than others, and the difference between them is not obvious from the outside. Closing that gap is exactly what careful evaluation is for.

Health

The best-evidenced interventions are relevant here

Several of the interventions with the strongest global evidence base — treating malaria, preventing malnutrition, routine vaccination — are directly relevant to Somali households. This is not a case where the research and the need sit in different places.

Careers and digital access

A generation deciding what to do with its working life

A generation coming online at once is a generation deciding what to do with their working lives. Career choice is one of the largest decisions most people ever make about their impact — and one of the least deliberately made.

How we think about choosing problems
Where we focus

Three areas, chosen deliberately

We can't work on everything, and a small community that tries to will do nothing well. These are the areas we point members toward — chosen because the evidence is unusually strong, the scale is large, or comparatively few people are working on them.

01

Global health

The strongest evidence base in all of effective giving. Decades of trials mean we can say with unusual confidence what a donation to malaria prevention or nutrition programmes actually buys — and much of that work happens in contexts close to our own.

02

AI safety

AI systems are being deployed faster than anyone is working out how to make them reliable, and very few people work on the problem relative to its scale. Somali technologists entering the field now are entering it early.

03

Animal welfare

An enormous amount of suffering, and among the most neglected areas in philanthropy — a small amount of funding and attention goes further here than almost anywhere else. It's a live question in a country where livestock is central to the economy.

Planned — not running yet

Digital literacy training

We intend to build a digital literacy programme teaching people to use technology productively and safely: practical skills for work, alongside the parts that rarely get taught — recognising scams and misinformation, protecting your own data, and understanding what AI tools can and can't be trusted to do. This is a plan, not a running programme. If you'd want to teach on it or take it, tell us — that's how we'll decide whether to build it.
